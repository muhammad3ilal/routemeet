Your planning data

RouteMeet stores saved groups and meetup choices in SQLite on the server running the app. Groups contain names, the starting-place text you entered and place identifiers. Saved meetups contain your label and the selected place identifier.

Your browser is your access key

A private browser cookie connects you to your saved items. There is no account or cross-device synchronization yet. Use the Delete buttons in Saved plans to remove your items. Clearing the cookie loses access but does not delete the database records. If this happens, the person operating the backend must handle deletion.

Map and route providers

In MapTiler mode, starting-place search queries go to MapTiler through the backend. The meeting-area center, search radius and category go to the configured Overpass service to retrieve mapped venues; individual names and typed addresses are not sent in that category query. Starting and candidate coordinates go to the configured OSRM routing server for driving estimates and routes. Map tiles and the MapTiler logo load directly from MapTiler in your browser. Participant names are not sent in these provider requests. When you open or share an Apple Maps link, its chosen destination and optional starting coordinates are sent to Apple or the recipient you select. Apple calculates its own route. QR codes are generated locally; no external QR service receives them.

See MapTiler’s privacy policy and the public routing service’s privacy and usage information. See also the Overpass service information. Custom routing or category servers have their own operators and policies. External providers may retain request logs under their policies.

Example mode uses fictional venues and synthetic travel times and makes no external map, search, or routing requests.

Retention

Saved items remain until deleted. Search results stay in backend memory for up to ten minutes and disappear on restart. Daily provider usage totals are persisted without place details. Server API keys are configured on the backend. The browser map key is public and origin-restricted; the server geocoding key remains on the backend.

The person operating the backend can access its database. Do not treat this prototype’s browser cookie as a substitute for a production account system.
